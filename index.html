A Dentist Explained Why Kids Get Cavities Even When They Brush Twice A Day, And It Has Nothing To Do With Effort

Published on November 7, 2026  ·  6 min read

Most parents are brushing their children's teeth completely wrong, and it isn't their fault. The real reason kids still get cavities has nothing to do with how hard anyone tries. Once you understand it, you can't unsee it.

Here's something almost no parent knows.

When your child brushes, even when you help, even when you do it for them, the brush only ever touches a small part of each tooth. The flat front. The top. That's it.

The sides, the gaps between teeth, and the line where the tooth meets the gum, the exact places cavities start, barely get touched at all.

It's not about effort. It's not about technique. A brush is a single small head being dragged by hand around a mouth full of tiny, awkward surfaces. It was never going to reach everything. It physically can't.

So every night, the easy parts get cleaned. And the parts that actually cause problems get missed. Again. And again. And again.

That's why children get cavities despite brushing twice a day. The damage grows in the places the brush never reached.

The Part That Surprises Every Parent

A dentist put it simply:

"Most parents think a missed cavity means they didn't brush well enough. It almost never does. It means the brush couldn't get where it needed to go. No parent can fix that by hand."

Read that again, because it changes everything.

It was never you. It was never your child being careless. It was the tool. A hand-held brush cleaning a fraction of the mouth and leaving the rest.

And once you see it that way, the nightly fight starts to look pointless. You're not failing at brushing. You're doing an impossible job with the wrong tool.

What's Really Happening Every Night

Picture tonight. 8 o'clock. Time to brush.

Your child pulls away before you even start. You hold their chin, you reach for the back ones, they squirm, they complain. A few minutes of scrubbing and you tell yourself it's done.

But you already know the truth. You didn't reach everything. You never do. Nobody could.

And the quiet worry sits there. Are their teeth actually clean, or does it just feel like the job's done?

Here's the uncomfortable part. Even a dentist, cleaning your child's teeth by hand with perfect technique, only reaches a fraction of the surfaces. If a professional can't do it by hand, a tired parent at the end of a long day was never going to.
